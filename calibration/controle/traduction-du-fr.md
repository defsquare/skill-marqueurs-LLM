# Observability: A Pillar of Modern Engineering

In a world where distributed systems are becoming the norm, observability is
establishing itself as a true cornerstone of software engineering. It is not
simply a matter of collecting logs — it is a matter of building a profound
understanding of how systems behave in production.

Observability rests on three fundamental pillars: logs, metrics and traces. Each
brings a particular light, and it is precisely their combination that makes it
possible to truly understand what is happening at the heart of the
infrastructure. In other words, none of these pillars is sufficient in isolation.

Logs constitute the historical building block of observability — they are
simple, universal and immediately usable. However, their volume can quickly
become problematic, particularly in microservice architectures where each
request traverses numerous services. It should be noted that the cost of storage
then becomes a major lever of arbitration.

Metrics, for their part, offer an aggregated view that is efficient and
inexpensive. They make it possible to detect anomalies, to follow trends and to
alert teams. On the other hand, they do not make it possible to explain an
anomaly — they merely signal it.

Distributed tracing, finally, brings the dimension that was missing: causality.
By following a request across the whole set of services, it becomes possible to
identify precisely the failing link. This is not a luxury, it is a necessity in
modern architectures.

In sum, observability is not a tool, it is a discipline. It demands real
investment, an adapted team culture and a permanent questioning of established
practices. At a time when systems are gaining in complexity, the organisations
that know how to invest in this discipline will undeniably have a considerable
advantage. Time will tell.
